# Features and methodology: results in plain words

Written 2026-10-09 from [FEATURESTUDY.md](FEATURESTUDY.md) (generated by `warwatch/featurestudy.py`). The test plan and pass rule were committed in [FEATURESTUDY_PLAN.md](FEATURESTUDY_PLAN.md) before any candidate was run. Nothing here changes the live page, `model_weights.json` or `events.csv`.

## What was done

1. **More events.** 83 past events from 2018 to 2026 were added in `warwatch/data/events_added.csv`. They were chosen from the public record by the codebook alone, without looking at any indicator, and each has a named source. With the current 78 that makes 161; 98 of them can be scored from 2021 on, against 51 before. The live model does not read this file.
2. **Nine changes tested against the published model**, each fixed in advance: three to the method (decayed conflict history, a global tempo term, boosted trees in place of the linear model) and six new features from data already held (slow GDELT build-up, cross-theatre attention share, smoothed evidence, oil and gold implied volatility with the VIX term ratio, travel-advisory steps, and the tanker with ISR or fighter co-occurrence).

## Result

- **No change beats the baseline under the rule set in advance.** The best Brier gains (implied volatility, slow build-up) have bootstrap p of 0.21 and 0.23, so they could easily be luck. Boosted trees are clearly worse. Nothing goes into the model.
- **The bigger finding is about the baseline itself.** On the extended list the published model no longer passes its own gates: Brier skill stays positive (+0.008) and the AUC interval stays above 0.5, but it no longer beats the shuffled-timing control (p = 0.22, needs below 0.05).
- **The probability does not rise before events.** Averaged over the scorable events, it sits at about 1.35 times the theatre's normal level from 60 days out to the day before, with no climb. It flags theatres that are often risky, not the weeks when something is coming. Within a theatre, its AUC is 0.54 (0.5 is chance).
- **On the 52 added events, which played no part in building the model, the day before an event looked like an ordinary day.** The median percentile is 46 against calm days, against 61 for the original events. Part of the original skill came from the seed list, not from a signal that carries over to new events.
- **Smoothed evidence is the one to watch.** It catches 33 events against 31 with half the false-alarm episodes per hit (1.2 against 2.4) and the highest AUC (0.641). Its Brier is level with the baseline (p = 0.47) and it was better in only 3 of 6 years, so it fails the rule and is not proven.

## What it means for the methodology

1. **Read the 30-day probability as a standing risk level per theatre, not as a countdown.** Within a theatre it does not time events.
2. **Adding the 83 events to `events.csv` would switch the model to shadow mode** at the next refit, because it would fail the shuffle gate, and the page would go back to the composite levels. That is the honest consequence of a larger yardstick, and it needs your decision.
3. **The data held today has been worked hard.** Six feature transformations here, six in `FEATURELAB.md`, eleven archive families in `LEADSTUDY.md` and the history variants in `IMPROVEMENTS.md`: none shows a lead that holds up. The limit is information, not the fitting method.
4. **What could change that is new information with years of history:** daily conflict-event data (ACLED and UCDP, tokens pending), wider GDELT event codes than the four roots held now, and the forward record as it grows.

## Caveats

- 98 scorable events in 13 theatres is still few, and a different event list would move every number.
- The ridge strength is chosen on the test years for the baseline and every candidate alike, which flatters all of them equally.
- Dates follow the seed list's convention: the local calendar day of the first attack.
